A kitchen sink full of greasy water is one of the most common plumbing calls, and it spikes around holidays. Roto-Rooter says the Friday after Thanksgiving ("Brown Friday") is often the busiest day of the year for plumbers. Based on its historical service data, it gets about 50% more calls that day than on an average Friday, and clogged kitchen sinks and jammed disposals top its list. Thanksgiving falls on November 26 in 2026. This guide draws mainly from InSinkErator's own troubleshooting pages, Roto-Rooter, NYC Sanitation (DSNY), and Angi cost data, linked at the bottom.

## First, narrow it down (1 minute)

- **Is it only the kitchen sink?** If only the kitchen sink is slow or backed up, the clog is probably in the sink drain or disposal, and you can often clear it yourself.
- **Are other drains acting up too?** Roto-Rooter lists warning signs of a main sewer line problem: several fixtures draining slowly, gurgling from toilets or drains, water backing up into a shower or tub when another fixture is used, clogs that keep coming back, and sewage smells. If you see these, stop using water and go to the [sewer backup guide](/sewer-backup-what-to-do-right-now/).
- **Does the disposal hum, or do nothing at all?** A hum usually means it's jammed. Total silence often means its overload protector tripped. Both are covered below. *(General guidance, consistent with InSinkErator's troubleshooting steps.)*
- **Apartment or condo?** A shared drain line may be the problem. Tell your landlord or building manager before calling a plumber yourself.

## Garbage disposal jammed or dead: the safe steps

These steps come from InSinkErator's support pages. Other brands are similar, but check your own manual.

1. **Turn the disposal switch off.** InSinkErator says to make sure the switch is off and to never put your hand in the disposal.
2. **Deal with standing water.** If the sink is full, wait for it to drain or bail it into a bucket or pot.
3. **Free the jam from underneath.** Most InSinkErator units have a hex socket dead center on the bottom of the unit, facing the floor. Insert the wrench that came with it (a small silver crank, about four inches long) or a standard **1/4-inch Allen wrench**. Work it back and forth in both directions until it turns freely in full circles. InSinkErator says it's OK to use some force, because the wrench is designed to bend before the disposal is damaged.
   - **EZ Connect units:** turn off the disposal and the water, then work the wrench in the center hole until it turns one full revolution.
   - **Builder Series, Amana, and ES units:** turn off the disposal and water, then rotate the turntable from above with a **wooden spoon** in either direction.
4. **Pull out what jammed it.** Shine a flashlight inside and look around the edge of the shredding disk. Remove anything you see with tongs or pliers, never your fingers.
5. **Reset the overload protector.** On most units it's a small **red square button** on the bottom of the disposal. If it has tripped, it sits about a quarter inch (5 mm) lower. Press it gently. If it won't stay up, wait ten minutes and try again.
   - On EZ Connect units with a wall switch, turn the switch off and let the disposal cool for up to 20 minutes. With a push-button air switch, unplug it for up to 20 minutes.
6. **Test it.** Turn on the cold water, then the disposal. If it jams again, repeat the wrench step. InSinkErator says you may need to do this several times.
7. **Still dead?** InSinkErator says to check your electrical panel for a tripped breaker or blown fuse.

**Call a plumber if** the disposal still won't run after a reset and a breaker check, it keeps tripping, or it leaks from the body of the unit. *(Our advice.)* Angi says that if a disposal needs replacing, parts alone run about $60 to $300, plus at least two hours of labor.

## Kitchen sink clogged (disposal or no disposal)

- **Stop running water** so the sink doesn't overflow. Bail out standing water into a bucket.
- **Try a plunger.** Angi suggests trying a plunger first on minor clogs before calling a pro. On a double sink, block the other drain with a wet rag or stopper so the pressure goes toward the clog, and if a dishwasher drains into the sink, clamp or kink its drain hose. *(Those two tips are general plumbing guidance, not from a cited source.)*
- **Hot water, not boiling.** For a slow drain, Angi suggests hot water to help move gunk along, but keep it under 140°F if you have plastic pipes.
- **Be careful with liquid drain cleaners.** Angi calls them harsh chemicals that can erode your pipes, and says bigger clogs need to be pulled out instead. If you've already poured some in, warn anyone who plunges or opens the pipes, since it can splash back. *(The splash warning is our advice.)*
- **Snake it or clean the trap** if you're comfortable doing it. Put a bucket under the U-shaped trap first. *(General guidance.)*

**Never do this:** don't keep running the dishwasher or disposal into a backed-up sink, and don't pour grease down the drain to "flush" a clog (see below).

## Call tonight, or wait until morning?

**Call tonight (or stop using water and call first thing) if:**

- Water is overflowing and you can't stop it
- Several drains are backing up at once, or sewage is coming up anywhere (see the [sewer backup guide](/sewer-backup-what-to-do-right-now/))
- Water is leaking under the sink into the cabinet or floor and you can't stop it. If it won't stop, shut off the water (see the [burst pipe guide](/burst-pipe-what-to-do-first-10-minutes/) for finding your main shutoff)

**It can usually wait until a regular-hours visit if** it's only the kitchen sink, it isn't overflowing, and you can wash dishes elsewhere for now. Night, weekend, and holiday calls usually cost more. *(Our general guidance; ask any plumber for their after-hours rate before they come out.)*

## Keep it from happening on Thanksgiving

- **Grease goes in the trash, not the drain.** Roto-Rooter says turkey drippings and oils go in liquid but cool and cling inside pipes. NYC Sanitation says to let oil and grease cool, pour it into a container, seal it, and throw it in the trash, then wipe pans with a paper towel.
- **Keep these out of the disposal** (Roto-Rooter): poultry bones and skin, potato peels, celery and corn husks, and large amounts of starchy food like potatoes, rice, and pasta. Scrape plates into the trash or compost first.
- **Run cold water.** InSinkErator says to grind with a moderate flow of cold water, keep it running a few seconds afterward, and avoid hot water while grinding. Roto-Rooter says to feed food in small amounts while the unit is running.
- **Wipes go in the trash,** even "flushable" ones. NYC Sanitation says they combine with grease to form "fatbergs" that cause sewer backups.
- **Fix slow drains before guests arrive.** Roto-Rooter notes that a drain that's already partly clogged can keep working until heavy holiday use blocks it completely.
