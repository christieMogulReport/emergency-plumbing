<div class="callout"><strong>Smell gas, or is anyone hurt?</strong> Leave the building and call 911 from outside. Don't flip switches or use a phone inside. A burst water pipe is a plumber problem; a gas leak is an emergency-services problem. <a href="/about/">Safety and disclaimer</a>.</div>

Water is spraying, dripping through the ceiling, or you hear it running with every tap closed. Do these steps in this order. They are drawn from guidance by insurers, the Red Cross, the CDC, and Ready.gov, linked at the bottom.

## Minute 0 to 2: Stop the water

1. **Close the main shutoff valve.** The Red Cross says that if water pipes are damaged, you should turn off the water at the main valve and call a plumber. [Progressive](https://www.progressive.com/answers/water-pipe-burst/) says the same: shut off the main supply to the whole house first.
2. **Where is it?** In cold climates it is usually inside, next to the water meter, often in a basement or utility closet where the water line enters the house. In warm climates the meter is often outside near the street and the house valve is where the line enters the home. [DisasterServices](https://disasterservices.com/water-damage/burst-pipe/) summarizes these utility and county guides.
3. **Know your valve type.** Per the same summary of water-district guidance: a round wheel handle (gate valve) turns clockwise until it stops and can take several full turns; a straight lever (ball valve) closes with a quarter turn. Don't force an old valve that won't move.
4. **Leave the city's valve alone.** The street-side valve at the meter (the "curb stop") belongs to the water utility, and DisasterServices notes Tampa's warning that only authorized utility staff may operate it. If you can't find your house valve or it won't close, call the water utility's emergency number. Reddit threads such as [this one on r/Plumbing](https://www.reddit.com/r/Plumbing/comments/1ht3miw/water_main_shutoffcannot_turn_it_off/) show this is a common problem (anecdotal): some commenters say the utility may come out for a major leak but can be slow, and suggest having a plumber install an easy-to-reach shutoff afterward.

## Minute 2 to 4: Electricity and the water heater

5. **Stay out of wet electricity.** Ready.gov: do not touch electrical equipment if it is wet or if you are standing in water. The [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/natural-disasters/response/what-to-do-protect-yourself-from-electrical-hazards.html) advises turning off power at the main breaker if circuits or equipment are wet or near water, and calling an electrician if you would have to enter standing water to reach the panel. Never switch power on or off while standing in water.
6. **Turn off the water heater.** For an electric heater, switch it off at the breaker. For a gas heater, set the control to off as the label on the unit shows. [Kentucky Farm Bureau](https://www.kyfb.com/insurance/lifes-blueprints/what-to-do-if-your-water-heater-leaks/) gives the same advice for leaking heaters. One reason to do it: with the water supply closed, an electric element left powered without water around it can burn out, which is why manufacturers such as A. O. Smith say the tank must be full before power is restored (as relayed by [Speedy Plumbing Baltimore](https://speedyplumbingbaltimore.com/guides/burst-pipe/)).
7. **If you can't safely reach the breaker panel, don't try.** Call an electrician or your electric utility.

## Minute 4 to 6: Drain the lines and limit the spread

8. **Open faucets and flush toilets.** [Progressive](https://www.progressive.com/answers/water-pipe-burst/) and [Hanover](https://www.hanover.com/resources/tips-individuals/prepare-now-learn-how/what-do-when-pipe-bursts) both say to open faucets and flush toilets to relieve pressure and drain what's left in the pipes. Start with the lowest faucet in the house so the water drains into a sink or tub instead of out of the break (Speedy Plumbing Baltimore).
9. **Move what you can.** Lift rugs, move furniture and electronics off wet floors, and put buckets under active drips.
10. **Start removing standing water only when it's safe.** Use towels, a mop, or a wet/dry vacuum. Progressive and Hanover both say fast drying (fans, dehumidifier) helps prevent mold.

## Minute 6 to 8: Document before you clean up

11. **Take photos and video first.** The National Association of Insurance Commissioners ([NAIC](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/publication-post-disaster-claims-guide.pdf)) tells policyholders to photograph or video damaged areas and belongings and jot notes on significant damage. FEMA's [floodsmart.gov](https://www.floodsmart.gov/recover/document-damage) adds: wide shots and close-ups, serial numbers on damaged electronics and appliances, and keep small samples of damaged materials.
12. **Photograph the burst pipe or failed part in place** before it is removed, if you can. If it's a water heater, [Kentucky Farm Bureau](https://www.kyfb.com/insurance/lifes-blueprints/what-to-do-if-your-water-heater-leaks/) says to keep the failed unit until the adjuster has inspected it.
13. **Keep every receipt.** NAIC says to keep all receipts for emergency repairs and to talk to your insurer before permanent repairs, because a company may not pay for repairs it didn't authorize.
14. **Call your insurer or agent.** Per [Progressive](https://www.progressive.com/answers/water-pipe-burst/), homeowners insurance may cover water damage from a sudden and accidental burst, but typically not damage from neglect or lack of maintenance. NAIC suggests knowing your deductible first: if the damage is clearly minor you may decide to pay out of pocket, but you may not see all the damage. Flood from outside (rising water) is different: Ready.gov notes homeowners policies do not cover flooding.

## Minute 8 to 10: Call a plumber

15. **Don't repair a burst pipe yourself.** Progressive and Hanover both advise calling a plumber rather than attempting the repair.
16. **Is it a "call now" emergency?** Active leaking you can't stop, a valve that won't close, sewage backing up into the home, or no usable water or toilet are reasons to call now. If you've shut the water off, the area is safe, and the plumbing can be left alone until morning, a business-hours visit is usually cheaper (see costs below).

### What to ask on the phone

- Are you licensed in my state or city? (Rules vary; you can usually check your state licensing board.)
- Is there an emergency or trip fee, and what is the hourly rate after hours? Does the trip fee get applied to the repair?
- Is there a minimum charge (for example one or two hours)?
- How soon can someone actually arrive, and will you call when you're on the way?
- Can I get the price or a written estimate before work begins?
- Will you keep the failed pipe or part and write on the invoice what failed? (Useful for the insurance claim.)

## What after-hours service typically costs

These are **national published estimates**, not quotes. Your price depends on your region, the time of day, and the repair.

| Source | What it says |
|---|---|
| [Angi, emergency plumber cost (updated Sep 10, 2026)](https://www.angi.com/articles/emergency-plumber-cost.htm) | Emergency plumbers charge about 1.5 to 3 times standard rates. Most pay $100 to $500 per hour, average about $170, up to $600 on holidays. Many add a flat emergency or trip fee of $150 to $300. |
| [Thumbtack, plumber cost (updated Nov 2, 2026)](https://www.thumbtack.com/p/plumbers-cost) | Emergency plumbing jobs typically $215 to $665, up to $1,516 at the high end. Burst pipe repair averages $234 to $771, up to $1,905. These are whole-job prices, not hourly rates. |

**What moves the bill** (Angi and Thumbtack): the time of the call (nights, weekends and holidays cost more, and holidays are highest), what actually failed and how hard it is to reach, local labor rates, and extras such as drywall repair or water damage restoration, which Angi says to budget for separately. A burst pipe inside a wall costs more than an exposed one because surfaces have to be opened and repaired.

## After the plumber leaves

- Keep fans and a dehumidifier running until the area is fully dry (Progressive, Hanover).
- Send your insurer the photos, receipts, and the plumber's invoice.
- Ask the plumber what caused the failure and whether insulation or a leak detection system would help (Progressive).
- Mark where the main shutoff is, and test it on a calm day so you aren't learning in an emergency (a tip from the City of Columbus water division, as reported by DisasterServices).
