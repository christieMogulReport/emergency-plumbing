The first hard freezes of the season bring the same question every year on X and Reddit: "I turned on the tap and nothing came out. Are my pipes frozen, and what do I do right now?" This guide is drawn from the American Red Cross and Angi, linked at the bottom.

## Is it a frozen pipe?

The [Red Cross](https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/types-of-emergencies/winter-storm/frozen-pipes.html) says: if you turn on a faucet and only a trickle comes out, suspect a frozen pipe. Other signs listed by [Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/what-do-if-your-pipes-freeze.htm):

- No water, or a slow trickle, at one or more fixtures
- Frost on the outside of a pipe
- A pipe that looks misshapen, bulging, or cracked
- Whistling or banging sounds in the pipes
- Dampness or water stains on walls or ceilings (this can mean it has already leaked)

**Where to look.** Pipes that freeze most often (Red Cross): outdoor hose bibs, pool and sprinkler lines, supply pipes in unheated basements, crawl spaces, attics, garages, and kitchen cabinets, and pipes running along poorly insulated exterior walls. Also check where the water service enters the house through the foundation. Angi suggests following the line back from each affected faucet and feeling for the coldest spot.

**Only one faucet, or the whole house?** If one sink is dry and the rest work, the freeze is probably on that fixture's line (often an exterior wall or cabinet). If nothing in the house has water, the freeze may be near where the line enters, or the problem may be the utility's side. Check with neighbors or your water utility before assuming it's inside your walls. *(This is general troubleshooting logic, not from a cited source.)*

## Step by step: thawing a pipe you can reach

1. **Know where your main shutoff is first.** Angi's first step is to locate the main water shutoff, because a pipe that split while frozen often starts leaking only once it thaws. Be ready to close it. If you can't find it, call your water utility's emergency line (Angi).
2. **Open the faucet on the frozen line.** Both the Red Cross and Angi say to keep it open. Angi says this relieves pressure on the unfrozen part of the pipe, and the Red Cross says flowing water helps melt the ice as it starts to thaw.
3. **Check for leaks.** If water is leaking from a crack anywhere on the pipe, it has already burst: shut off the main and call a plumber (Angi). Switch to the [burst pipe guide](/burst-pipe-what-to-do-first-10-minutes/).
4. **Apply gentle heat** to the frozen section. Safe options from the Red Cross: an electric heating pad wrapped around the pipe, a hair dryer, a portable space heater kept away from anything flammable, or towels soaked in hot water. Angi recommends a hair dryer on its lowest setting.
5. **Keep heating until full water pressure comes back** (Red Cross).
6. **Check every other faucet.** If one pipe froze, others may have too (Red Cross).

### Never do this

- **No open flame.** The Red Cross says not to use a blowtorch, kerosene or propane heater, charcoal stove, or any other open-flame device. Angi warns a torch can start a house fire or make the pipe burst.
- **Don't pour boiling water** on the pipe or down the drain (Angi).
- **Mind the electricity.** Angi says to watch where the melting ice goes so you don't create standing water near your hair dryer or heater. Don't use electrical devices while standing in water.

## When to stop and call a plumber

Call a licensed plumber if (Red Cross and Angi):

- You can't find the frozen section, or it's inside a wall, ceiling, or under a slab
- You can't get it to thaw
- The pipe is cracked, bulging, or leaking, or starts leaking as it thaws

After a big freeze, plumbers are often swamped. Angi notes it may be days before a pro can come, and says a small pinhole leak can sometimes be held temporarily with a pipe repair clamp or patch until then. Keep the water off at the main if the leak can't be controlled.

**Is it a "call tonight" emergency?** Active leaking you can't stop, a valve that won't close, or no usable water at all are reasons to call now. If nothing is leaking, the main is off or the frozen line is isolated, and you can wait until morning, a business-hours visit is usually cheaper than an after-hours call (see [costs in our burst pipe guide](/burst-pipe-what-to-do-first-10-minutes/)).

### What to ask on the phone

- Do you thaw frozen lines, and is that a flat fee or hourly?
- Is there an after-hours or trip fee on top?
- How soon can someone actually get here?
- If the pipe has split, can you repair it on the same visit?

## Keep it from happening again tonight

From the Red Cross unless noted:

- Let cold water drip from faucets served by exposed pipes. Even a trickle helps prevent freezing.
- Open kitchen and bathroom cabinet doors so warm air reaches the pipes (move cleaners out of children's reach).
- Keep garage doors closed if water lines run through the garage.
- Keep the thermostat at the same temperature day and night during a cold snap. If you're away, leave the heat on, no lower than 55°F.
- Before winter: disconnect and drain outdoor hoses, close the inside valve to the hose bib and leave the outside tap open to drain, and insulate pipes in attics, basements, crawl spaces, and under sinks (pipe sleeves or UL-listed heat tape).
- Angi adds sealing cracks that let cold air reach pipes.
