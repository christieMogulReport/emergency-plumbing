Almost every emergency on this site starts with the same instruction: *shut off the main water*. That is exactly the moment many people discover the valve is seized, the handle just spins, or they have no idea where it is. "Main shutoff valve stuck" is one of the most common questions on Reddit's r/homeowners and r/askaplumber, and a busy holiday weekend with a full house is a bad time to find out. This guide draws on water utilities (City of Salem, Oregon; City of Grand Prairie, Texas; Aquarion Water), plumber guidance, and Angi cost data, all linked at the bottom.

## Your house has (at least) two places to stop the water

| Valve | Where it usually is | Who operates it |
|---|---|---|
| **Main (house) valve** | Inside, where the water line enters the house: basement wall, crawl space, garage, utility room, near the water heater, or in a closet on the street-facing side. Aquarion calls it the cellar or house valve and says it's normally at the service line's point of entry. | You. This is the one to use first. |
| **Meter or curb valve** | Outside, usually in a meter box near the street or sidewalk. Grand Prairie describes a covered box labeled WATER near the curb; Salem says most meters are in a box in front of the property, and Aquarion describes the curb box cover as a round plate about 3 inches across. | Often you can, with the right key. In some areas only the utility should. Check below. |

Some homes also have a valve on the house side of the meter, inside the meter box. Salem says that if there's a hand-operated valve on the customer side of the meter, you can use it; if not, there's always a curb stop valve on the street side, which needs a curb stop key.

## If the main valve won't turn: don't force it

The most common culprit is an old **gate valve**, the kind with a round wheel handle. Plumbers warn that the stem in these valves can corrode, and forcing it with a wrench or pliers can snap the stem or break the gate off inside, leaving you with a valve that can't be closed at all ([Washington Plumbing & Drain](https://www.washington-plumbing.com/problems/main-shutoff-valve-wont-turn/)). The City of Grand Prairie's advice is blunt: old valves can be damaged and corroded, so only use your hand to turn them, and call a plumber if you can't.

- **Wheel (gate) valve:** turn clockwise by hand. If it doesn't move with firm hand pressure, stop.
- **Lever (ball) valve:** a quarter turn. Grand Prairie says the lever parallel to the pipe means ON, and perpendicular (90°) means OFF.
- **Handle spins freely, or the valve "closes" but faucets still run:** the gate has likely separated from the stem. It won't close no matter how far you turn it. Go to the meter or curb valve.
- **Valve drips from around the stem after you turn it:** a small weep at the packing nut is common on old valves. Put a bucket or towel under it and have it looked at; don't keep cranking.

**Go to the street-side valve instead.** That's the safer move than wrestling a seized indoor valve while water sprays.

## Using the meter or curb valve

1. **Turn off the water heater first if you can.** Salem's first step for shutting off water service is to shut off the water heater to avoid damaging it. Gas: set the control to "pilot" or "off." Electric: switch off its breaker if you can reach the panel from a dry spot. Our [water heater guide](/water-heater-leaking-what-to-do-tonight/) covers this.
2. **Open the meter box lid carefully.** Grand Prairie says you can lift it with a screwdriver or a meter key, and warns that the lid may be attached by a wire to the meter's reading device, which is costly to repair if broken. Watch for spiders, snakes, and other critters in the box.
3. **Find the valve.** Use a customer-side hand valve if there is one (Salem). Otherwise the curb stop on the street side needs a **curb stop key** or meter key, which Grand Prairie and Salem say hardware stores sell.
4. **Turn it slowly.** A typical curb or meter valve is a quarter-turn; stop when it stops. Don't hammer or use a cheater bar.
5. **Confirm it worked.** Open a faucet; the flow should taper off within a minute or so as the pressure drains.

**Check your local rules.** Some utilities don't allow customers to operate the curb valve or meter, and tampering rules vary. If you're unsure, can't find the box, or the key won't fit, **call your water utility's emergency or after-hours line** (it's usually on your water bill or the utility's website). Many utilities, like Salem's Public Works Dispatch, staff a 24-hour line and will come shut the water off at the street.

## Water utility or plumber?

Aquarion's "who owns what" explanation is typical: the utility owns the water main and the connection up to the curb valve, and the homeowner owns the service line from the curb valve into the house, plus the house valve. In general:

- **Call the water utility** to shut water off at the street, if the leak is at or before the meter, or if water is bubbling up in the yard or street.
- **Call a plumber** for anything on your side: a broken or seized main valve, a leaking service line, or a burst pipe inside. Rules differ by utility, so check yours.

Once the water is off at the street, tonight's emergency is contained. Replacing a stuck main valve can usually wait for business hours and avoid night and holiday premiums; see [what an after-hours plumber costs](/emergency-plumber-cost-after-hours-holiday/).

## What a main valve replacement typically costs

These are **published national estimates, not quotes.** Nights, weekends, and holidays usually cost more.

- **Replacing a main water shutoff valve:** Angi (updated October 2026) puts the national average at about **$500**, with most homeowners paying **$375 to $600**, and up to about $800 for stainless steel valves that require trenching.
- **Permits:** Angi says to budget **$150 to $500** for permits, plus trenching if the valve is buried.
- **The valve itself:** Angi lists ball and gate valves at roughly $10 to $50 for the part; most of the cost is labor and access.

Plumbers commonly recommend replacing an old gate valve with a quarter-turn ball valve, which is quicker to close in an emergency.

## Do this before the next emergency (10 minutes, tomorrow)

- **Find and label your main valve** so anyone in the house can shut it off. Hang a tag on it.
- **Test it gently by hand.** If it won't move by hand, don't force it; put "replace main valve" on your list for a daytime plumber visit.
- **Find the meter box** and check whether your utility lets customers use the curb valve. If so, keep a meter or curb key where you can find it.
- **Use the meter as a leak check.** Grand Prairie notes that if no water is being used and the meter is still moving, you have a leak somewhere.
- **Know the after-hours number** for your water utility and save it in your phone.
