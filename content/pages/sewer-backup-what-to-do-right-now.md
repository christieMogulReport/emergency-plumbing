Water coming up through the basement floor drain, a toilet gurgling when the washer drains, or the tub filling with dirty water when someone flushes: these are classic signs of a **sewer backup**, and they show up again and again in plumbing forums and on social media, especially after heavy rain and around holidays when houses are full. This guide is drawn mainly from city sewer departments (Madison, WI; Roseville, CA; Davis, CA), the Illinois Department of Public Health, the Michigan insurance regulator, and Angi cost data, linked at the bottom.

## Is it a sewer backup or just one clogged drain?

| What you see | Likely problem |
|---|---|
| One sink, one toilet, or one tub is slow or stopped, everything else drains fine | A local clog in that fixture's drain. Usually not an emergency. For a single clogged or overflowing toilet, see [Toilet Overflowing or Clogged?](/toilet-overflowing-clogged-what-to-do-now/) |
| **Several drains back up at once**, toilets or drains gurgle, water comes up through a floor drain, or sewage backs up into a tub or shower | Likely the main sewer line or the public sewer (City of Roseville's list of backup signs) |
| Backup appears at the **lowest drain in the house** (basement floor drain, basement toilet or shower) | Typical of a backup. The City of Madison says sewage usually pools around a floor drain, toilet, or shower on the lowest level. |

A plumber's quick test from a [r/Plumbing thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/Plumbing/comments/128sb2b/standing_watersewage_in_basement_floor_drain/): run water in a nearby bathroom and watch the floor drain. If the water in the floor drain rises, the shared line downstream is blocked (anecdotal, one person's advice).

## Step by step: the first 15 minutes

1. **Stop using water. All of it.** Every flush, shower, dishwasher, and washing-machine cycle sends more water into a pipe that can't drain, and it comes up at the lowest opening, often your basement. Roseville says to turn off washers, dishwashers, showers, and sinks immediately. Tell everyone in the house.
2. **Call your city or sewer utility's sewer line before (or while) you call a plumber.** Many cities run a 24/7 sewer emergency line and will check the public main for free. Roseville, CA says it assesses at no charge and clears the blockage if it's in the city system; Davis, CA says it fixes problems in the city-maintained part at no cost to the resident; Madison, WI dispatches crews 24 hours a day. Look up "[your city] sewer backup" or check your water/sewer bill for the number. If the problem is in the city main, this can save you a plumber's after-hours fee entirely.
3. **Keep people and pets away and limit the spread.** Block doorways with towels if it's safe (Roseville). Close the door to the affected room.
4. **Watch what happens when the city crew clears the main.** Madison explains that if the backed-up sewage drains back down the floor drain after the crew clears the city main, the main was the problem. If it stays, the blockage is in your **private sewer lateral** (the pipe from your house to the street) and you need a plumber or drain-cleaning service.
5. **Call your insurance company** if you have sewer backup coverage (Madison). Photograph everything before you clean up.

### Don't do this

- **Don't keep plunging the toilet or pouring drain cleaner** into a main-line backup. If the main is blocked, the water has nowhere to go, and chemical drain cleaner makes the water caustic for you and the plumber.
- **Don't run the washer "just to finish the load."** Laundry water is one of the biggest volumes a house sends down the drain.
- **Don't wade through sewage barefoot or touch it with bare hands**, especially with cuts (Illinois Department of Public Health).

## Who's responsible: the city or you?

In most places the split looks like this (Roseville and Madison):

- **City:** the sewer main in the street and, in some cities, the service connection it maintains. The city clears blockages there.
- **Property owner:** the **sewer lateral** from the building to the city main, including clearing clogs in it and repairing it if it's broken or full of roots.

Davis, CA notes that if there's no accessible cleanout on your lateral (buried under landscaping or concrete, or none at all), you'll need a plumber, and that city staff can't recommend plumbers. Madison says homeowners are responsible for cleanup on their property, but you can **file a claim with the city** if you believe the city main caused the backup. Rules vary, so ask your city.

## Cleaning up safely

Sewage can contain bacteria, viruses, and other organisms that cause disease (Illinois Department of Public Health). For a small, contained backup:

- **Wear rubber boots, rubber gloves, and eye protection** (CDC). IDPH also recommends an N95 respirator during cleanup. Cover any cuts with a waterproof bandage.
- **Turn off power to the area if needed**, but only if you can reach the panel without standing in water (IDPH). Let wet appliances and outlets dry and be checked before using them.
- **Open windows and run fans** to ventilate and dry (IDPH).
- **Throw out anything contaminated that can't be thoroughly washed.** Carpets and rugs that can't be fully cleaned and dried should be discarded (IDPH).
- **Wash hard surfaces with soap and warm water, then disinfect** with no more than 1 cup of bleach per gallon of water. **Never mix bleach with ammonia**; the fumes are dangerous (IDPH).
- **Wash your hands** right after contact and before eating. Keep children out until it's cleaned (IDPH).

For a large backup, a finished basement, or soaked drywall, a professional water-damage cleanup company is usually worth it. As our [water heater guide](/water-heater-leaking-what-to-do-tonight/) notes, mold can start within 24 to 48 hours in wet building materials.

## Call tonight, or wait until morning?

**Call tonight** (city line first, then a plumber if it's your lateral) if:

- Sewage is actively coming up a drain or toilet, or spreading into living space
- You can't avoid using water (young children, someone who is ill, no other bathroom)
- Sewage is near electrical equipment, a furnace, or a water heater

**It can often wait for business hours** if the city has cleared its main, the backup has drained away, and you can get by with no water use until a plumber comes in the morning. Avoiding a night or weekend call-out can save money; see the after-hours rates in our [burst pipe guide](/burst-pipe-what-to-do-first-10-minutes/).

### What to ask the plumber on the phone

- Is there an after-hours or trip fee, and is it credited toward the work?
- Will you clear the line from an outdoor cleanout, or do you need to pull a toilet?
- Is the quoted price for snaking (rodding) or hydro jetting?
- If it's roots or a broken pipe, can you run a camera and give me a copy of the video? (Madison recommends keeping a copy if backups recur.)

## What it typically costs

These are **published national estimates from [Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/cost-to-clear-a-main-sewer-line-clog.htm) (updated Oct 8, 2026), not quotes.** Nights, weekends, and holidays usually cost more.

| Job | Published estimate |
|---|---|
| Main sewer line clog repair | National average $380; most pay $188 to $574 (based on 1,037 Angi projects) |
| Snaking (rodding) a main line | $100 to $250 |
| Hydro jetting | $350 to $600 |
| Sewer camera inspection, if needed | $200 to $1,300 or more |
| Tree root removal | $100 to $1,000 |
| General line repairs | $1,000 to $4,000 |
| Full sewer pipe replacement | $3,000 to $25,000 |

If the city finds the clog in its main, clearing it is usually free (Roseville, Davis).

## Will insurance cover it?

Often **not by default.** The [Michigan Department of Insurance and Financial Services](https://www.michigan.gov/difs/-/media/Project/Websites/difs/Disaster_Prep/FIS-PUB_6380.pdf) says water and sewer backup coverage is **not automatically included** in homeowners or renters policies; it's an add-on you buy, and it pays only up to the limit you chose. It typically **doesn't cover flood** damage or repair of a broken sump pump. If you have a finished basement or store things there, ask your agent about it before you need it.

## Preventing the next one

- **Know where your sewer cleanout is** and keep it accessible (Davis notes crews may not be able to help if it's buried under landscaping or concrete).
- **Don't pour grease down the kitchen drain** or flush wipes. Angi suggests inexpensive drain strainers on kitchen and tub drains.
- **If backups keep happening,** have the lateral camera-inspected for roots or a break (Madison).
- **Save your city's 24/7 sewer number in your phone now.** Roseville makes the same suggestion.
- **Basement water that's groundwater, not sewage?** If the sump pit is overflowing or the pump won't run, see [Sump Pump Stopped Working or the Power Is Out?](/sump-pump-not-working-basement-flooding/)
- Know where your main water shutoff is too. See the [burst pipe guide](/burst-pipe-what-to-do-first-10-minutes/) and, in cold weather, [frozen pipes](/frozen-pipes-how-to-thaw-safely/).
