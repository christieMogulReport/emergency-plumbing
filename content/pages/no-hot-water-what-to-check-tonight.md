"Finally home and the shower has no hot water." Posts like that show up on X every night once the weather turns cold. Often the cause is a tripped breaker, a tripped safety switch, or a pilot light that went out, and you can check all three yourself in a few minutes. This guide is drawn mainly from A. O. Smith's troubleshooting guide, Lennox, the Kansas Corporation Commission, and Angi cost data, linked at the bottom.

## First, narrow it down (2 minutes)

- **Is it every hot tap, or just one?** If the kitchen has hot water but one shower doesn't, the water heater is probably fine. A. O. Smith notes that faulty shower controls or a bad thermostatic mixing valve can cause this. That's a fixture repair, not an emergency.
- **Is it no hot water, or just not enough?** Lukewarm water that runs out fast can point to a failed heating element on an electric heater or a thermocouple problem on a gas one (Angi). A. O. Smith also notes that colder incoming water in winter and heavy use can cause "not enough hot water" complaints.
- **Did the power just go out or flicker?** Lennox says outages and power surges can trip an electric heater's reset button.
- **Any water on the floor around the tank?** If so, stop here and follow the [water heater leak guide](/water-heater-leaking-what-to-do-tonight/).
- **Gas or electric?** A gas heater has a gas pipe and a vent pipe on top. An electric heater has a cable going into it and no flue *(general identification tip, not from a cited source)*.
- **Apartment or shared building?** If the whole building is out, the problem is the building's system. Tell your landlord or building manager rather than calling a plumber yourself.

## Electric tank heater: what to check

1. **Check the breaker.** Angi says a tripped breaker should always be your first check: it costs nothing and takes a minute. Look for the breaker labeled "water heater" or "hot water." If it has tripped, switch it fully off, then back on.
2. **Press the reset button** (Lennox's steps):
   - Turn **off** the breaker to the water heater first.
   - Remove the upper access panel with a screwdriver.
   - Find the red reset button on the thermostat and press it firmly. A click means it had tripped.
   - Put the panel back and turn the breaker back on.
   - Wait 30 to 60 minutes for the water to heat up, then check.
3. **What the click tells you.** A. O. Smith says that if the button clicks, the water got too hot and tripped the high-limit switch (it calls this the Energy Cut Off). Lennox calls it the high-limit safety switch. It's a safety device, not just a restart button.
4. **If the breaker trips again or the reset won't hold,** stop resetting it. Lennox lists common causes: a shorted heating element, a stuck thermostat, loose or corroded wiring, a failing breaker, or water reaching electrical parts. A. O. Smith's next steps involve testing elements and thermostats with a meter on live 240-volt equipment. That's a job for a plumber or electrician.

**Never do this:** don't keep resetting a button or breaker that keeps tripping (Lennox says repeated resets without fixing the cause can make things worse), and don't open the panel without turning off the breaker first.

## Gas tank heater: what to check

**Sniff first.** If you smell rotten eggs, don't relight anything. The [Kansas Corporation Commission](https://www.kcc.ks.gov/natural-gas-pipeline-safety-liquid-pipeline/natural-gas-safety) says to leave right away, avoid anything that could spark (light switches, phones, garage door openers), don't try to find the leak or shut off appliance gas valves yourself, and call the utility or 911 once you're somewhere safe.

If there's no gas smell:

1. **Look for the pilot.** A. O. Smith says the pilot can be hard to see, so dim the room lights. Newer A. O. Smith-style electronic valves have a status light. If it isn't flashing, the pilot is probably out.
2. **Relight it using the instructions on the heater's label.** Lennox's outline: turn the gas control to OFF, **wait several minutes** for leftover gas to clear, turn the knob to PILOT, light it following the label, then turn it to ON once the pilot stays lit. A. O. Smith says to hold the control knob in for 60 seconds after the pilot lights (90 seconds on its electronic valves) before releasing.
3. **If the pilot won't light at all,** A. O. Smith says to make sure the gas supply valve to the heater is on and the control knob is pushed all the way in. If the igniter sparks but nothing lights, it's likely a gas supply problem. If you also have no gas at the stove or furnace, call your gas utility.
4. **If the pilot lights but goes out when you let go,** A. O. Smith says to press the reset button on the **thermal switch** (if your model has one). A click means it tripped. A. O. Smith says that usually means **the heater isn't getting enough air**, from a clogged air filter or flame arrestor or a venting problem.
5. **If it trips again, or the pilot still won't stay lit,** turn the gas control to OFF and call a pro. Angi says a failed thermocouple is the most likely cause when the pilot is lit but the burner won't light, and says gas valve replacement is never a DIY job because a leaking valve can cause an explosion.

**A thermal switch that keeps tripping is a warning, not a nuisance.** It means combustion or venting isn't right, so don't keep resetting it. Leave the heater off until it's inspected *(our advice, based on A. O. Smith's explanation of what the switch does)*.

## Tankless heaters

Tankless units show error codes on their display. Look up the code in the owner's manual or on the manufacturer's website before doing anything else. Angi notes that tankless repairs, especially on condensing units, can cost more than tank repairs because they have more components. *(Error-code advice is general guidance, not from a cited source.)*

## Call tonight, or wait until morning?

**Call tonight** (or your gas utility first) if:

- You smell gas, or hear hissing near the heater
- The breaker trips again right after you reset it, or you smell burning (Lennox lists a burning smell or unusual noises as reasons to call a pro)
- Water is leaking near the burner or electrical parts (see the [leak guide](/water-heater-leaking-what-to-do-tonight/))
- Someone in the home truly can't go without hot water tonight (for example, for medical needs)

**It can usually wait** if nothing smells or leaks and the heater is just cold. Angi says that if you can go without hot water for an extra day, scheduling the visit during the work week helps, and that winter is the busiest season for water heater repair. Night and weekend calls cost more; see the after-hours rates in our [burst pipe guide](/burst-pipe-what-to-do-first-10-minutes/). Meanwhile, you can heat water on the stove for washing up.

### What to ask on the phone

- Is there an after-hours or trip fee, and does it go toward the repair?
- I have a [gas / electric / tankless] heater, about [age] years old. Do you carry common parts (thermocouple, elements, thermostats) on the truck?
- If it needs replacing, can you bring one the same size and fuel type?
- Is the heater still under warranty? (Angi says to check, and notes that DIY repairs or skipped maintenance can void some warranties.)

## What it typically costs

These are **published national estimates from Angi, not quotes**. Your price depends on your area, the time of the call, and the heater.

| Repair (Angi, updated Aug 24, 2026) | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Tripped breaker (reset it yourself) | $0 |
| Pilot light won't stay lit (service call, mostly trip fee) | About $50 to $150 |
| Thermocouple replacement | About $150 or less |
| Electric heating element replacement | About $200 to $300 |
| Electric thermostat replacement | About $150 to $200 |
| Gas valve replacement | About $350 on average |
| Any water heater repair (national average, 16,111 projects) | $616 average; most pay $228 to $1,017 |
| Full tank replacement | About $850 to $1,800 |

Angi says conventional tank heaters last 8 to 12 years. If yours is near that age and needs a major repair, replacing it may make more sense.

## Once it's working again

- A. O. Smith and Lennox both suggest setting the thermostat around 120°F. A. O. Smith says if you set it higher, install a thermostatic mixing valve to reduce scalding risk.
- Flush the tank to remove sediment (Lennox, Angi). Angi suggests twice a year for tank heaters, more often with very hard water.
- On gas heaters with an air filter or flame arrestor, keep it clean and keep storage boxes, laundry, and lint away from the heater (A. O. Smith links clogs to thermal switch trips).
- Label the water heater breaker and the cold-water valve, and learn where your main water shutoff is (see the [burst pipe guide](/burst-pipe-what-to-do-first-10-minutes/)).
